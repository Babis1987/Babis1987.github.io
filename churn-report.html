Customer Churn Prediction

Logistic Regression, Neural Networks and CatBoost with SHAP Interpretability

Summary

The study builds a customer-churn predictor for a telecom operator on the Telco Customer Churn dataset (Kaggle). A tuned logistic regression is used as the baseline and is compared with a multi-layer perceptron (with two strategies for the class imbalance) and with CatBoost. All models are tuned for the F1-score, because only about a quarter of the customers churn and accuracy alone would reward a model that ignores them. The final model is then explained with SHAP.

Main findings

  • CatBoost is the strongest model: F1 0.6247 and ROC-AUC 0.8360 on the test set, ahead of the neural network (0.6189 / 0.8351) and logistic regression (0.6015 / 0.8287).
  • The gains over the linear baseline are small. The neural network improves F1 by 1.74 points and recall by about 3.5 points; CatBoost adds another 0.6 points of F1. The report reads this as evidence that the relationships behind churn are mainly linear.
  • SMOTE did not help. Oversampling the minority class lowered the network's F1 to 0.5838 (from 0.6189 with class weights), probably because interpolated samples add noise at the class boundary.
  • Three factors dominate the churn decision in the SHAP analysis: the contract type, the monthly charges and the tenure. Month-to-month contracts and fiber optic service push customers towards leaving; long tenure protects against it.

1. Data and preprocessing

The Telco Customer Churn dataset contains 7,043 customers with 20 features in four groups: demographic (gender, SeniorCitizen, Partner, Dependents), service usage (PhoneService, InternetService, OnlineSecurity and others), financial (MonthlyCharges, TotalCharges) and contract (Contract, PaymentMethod, PaperlessBilling). Most features are categorical with two or three levels. The target is imbalanced: 73.5% of customers stay and 26.5% churn.

01

Cleaning

customerID dropped (no predictive value). TotalCharges converted to numeric; its 11 missing values (<0.2%) removed.

02

Encoding

Two-level variables (including Churn) mapped to 0/1; multi-level variables one-hot encoded with drop_first=True.

03

Redundancy filter

Feature pairs with correlation above 0.99 removed: TotalCharges and the “no internet service” columns. 8 of 30 columns dropped.

04

Iterative VIF

While any VIF > 10, keep the feature most correlated with churn and drop the rest; 2 more columns removed, leaving 20.

05

Split and scale

Stratified 80/20 split (73.4% / 26.6% in both parts); StandardScaler fitted on the training set only.

Linear or non-linear? Pearson against Spearman

Before modelling, every feature was correlated with the target with both Pearson (linear) and Spearman (monotonic) coefficients. A small gap between the two points to linear relationships, which favour logistic regression. The two rankings are almost identical; only tenure (−0.354 / −0.370), TotalCharges (−0.199 / −0.232) and MonthlyCharges (0.193 / 0.184) differ at all.

After the redundancy filter and the VIF loop, the largest absolute correlation between the 20 remaining features is 0.5638, well below the 0.8 target.

2. Logistic regression baseline

The baseline was tuned with GridSearchCV and 5-fold stratified cross-validation over 24 hyperparameter combinations (penalty, C, solver, max_iter) with class_weight='balanced', scored by F1. The best configuration reached a cross-validated F1 of 0.6303.

On the test set the model catches 289 of the 374 churners (77.3%) and misses 85 (22.7%), at the cost of 298 false alarms: high recall, lower precision.

The coefficients give the direction of each effect directly. Fiber optic service, electronic-check payment and paperless billing raise the probability of churn; tenure and one- or two-year contracts lower it.

Its strengths are speed, low cost and, above all, interpretability. Its weakness is that it cannot capture non-linear interactions without hand-crafted features, which motivates the more flexible models below.

3. Neural network (MLP)

A Keras multi-layer perceptron with a searchable depth and width: 1–3 hidden blocks of Dense → Batch Normalization → ReLU → Dropout, L2 regularisation on every dense layer and a two-neuron softmax output. Optuna (TPE sampler) searched the space below, scoring each trial by mean F1 over 5 stratified folds, with a Median Pruner to stop weak trials early. Training used Adam, early stopping and ReduceLROnPlateau.

4. CatBoost

CatBoost (gradient boosting on oblivious trees with ordered target statistics) was tuned with the same Optuna protocol, maximising F1 over 5 stratified folds. The search covered tree depth, learning rate, number of iterations and L2 leaf regularisation, and the model used CatBoost's native handling of categorical features. Ten Optuna trials took 3.8 minutes.

  • F1: CatBoost 0.6247, neural network 0.6189, logistic regression 0.6015, neural network with SMOTE 0.5838.
  • Recall versus precision: the class-weighted network finds the most churners (recall 0.8075) at the price of more false positives; CatBoost gives the most balanced behaviour.
  • Cost: logistic regression ran 120 grid-search fits in 8.15 s on a CPU; CatBoost's 10-trial search took 3.8 minutes; the neural networks were the most expensive and benefit from a GPU.
The report draws two conclusions. With only about two F1 points between the linear model and the non-linear ones, logistic regression is the most rational choice for this dataset today: almost as accurate, fastest and fully transparent. For the next phase, when richer customer data (complaint logs, usage history) would add complexity beyond a linear model, CatBoost is selected as the most flexible option, and it is the model explained below.

Direction of the effects

Month-to-month contracts have strongly positive SHAP values and sharply raise the risk of leaving. Tenure acts the other way: long-standing customers sit on the negative side. Fiber optic users show a higher tendency to leave, possibly because of cost or technical problems.

One customer, explained

The waterfall starts from the model's average output and adds each feature's contribution for one customer. For this customer the contract and the tenure pull the prediction strongly down, while the monthly charges push it up.

7. Conclusions and recommendations

The SHAP analysis points to three drivers of churn: flexible (month-to-month) contracts, high monthly charges and service quality, with fiber optic connections often appearing as a source of dissatisfaction. The report proposes three actions:

Contract migration

Target month-to-month customers with discounts for moving to an annual commitment.

Fiber optic quality control

Investigate fiber-related complaints and review the pricing policy.

Cheap retention for false positives

Because the model raises many false alarms (260 for CatBoost), retention campaigns should be low-cost (newsletters, preferential service) rather than aggressive discounts to loyal customers.

Next steps

  • Enrich the data with call history, usage logs and complaint texts to capture behaviour beyond basic demographics.
  • Collect more customers: boosting models scale well to tens or hundreds of thousands of records.
  • Explore hybrids of neural networks and gradient boosting to reduce classification errors further.
